Frankfurt trip research

Everything gathered for the Frankfurt trip, Dec 24, 2026 to Jan 12, 2027. Late December in Frankfurt hovers around freezing and is often wet. Four parts: the timeline, the luggage plan, warm clothes for walking around in the cold, and a clothing cart already put together.

Austin is on Central Time (CT, UTC−6). Frankfurt is on Central European Time (CET, UTC+1). Frankfurt is 7 hours ahead.

This trip is about time together; everything else is optional.

Things you didn't ask about but should know

  1. The fare is carry-on only (8 kg), so anything heavy gets bought there. A checked bag would add roughly $120 each way. Blankets, warm clothes, and pantry stock are cheaper to buy in Frankfurt than to fly over.
  2. Medications (arriving from the US): a personal supply for the trip is allowed. Keep it in the original labeled containers, in the carry-on, with a copy of the prescription or a short doctor's letter in English. Controlled drugs (opioids, ADHD stimulants, some sleep and anxiety medicines) need a doctor-completed certificate. Check whether any of yours count, well before December. Don't carry medicines for anyone else, or anything unprescribed.
  3. Protect sleep on both travel days. The +7 h eastbound shift is the biggest health risk of the trip. Use the jet-lag windows in the timeline, and ask the doctor about timing meds across the shift.
  4. Germany closes on holidays and Sundays. Shops and supermarkets are shut Dec 25, Dec 26, Jan 1, and every Sunday. They close early on Dec 31. Arrival day is a holiday, so groceries must be bought before. Train-station and airport shops stay open. Each area has one rotating emergency pharmacy ("Apotheken-Notdienst").
  5. The fare is Economy Light: no changes and no refunds. Travel insurance with trip-interruption and medical coverage is worth more than usual here.
  6. Entry rules for US citizens: no visa for stays up to 90 days. The EU Entry/Exit System (EES) is live, so expect fingerprints and a photo at the first border, which means a longer line. ETIAS is not required yet (expected 2027); re-check in December on the official site only.
  7. Train strikes: the rail unions' no-strike periods ran out in early 2026, so strikes are possible again. They're usually announced a day or more ahead. Check the DB Navigator app before any train day.
  8. Short days: roughly 8 hours of daylight, sunrise around 8:20 am and dark by about 4:30 pm. Put walks and outings at midday.
  9. New Year's Eve in Germany means fireworks everywhere at midnight, very loud and crowded, with packed transit. Enjoy it, or watch from a window.
  10. Emergency number: 112 (ambulance and fire). Police: 110.

2. Cold-weather clothing

Sizes: XL tops, 40x30 pants. The everyday preference is cotton, but the research is clear on one point: cotton is the worst fabric against the skin in cold, wet weather. It soaks up sweat and rain, holds it, and pulls heat out of the body once it's damp. Merino wool stays warm when damp, wicks moisture, dries faster, and doesn't smell after days of wear. So the plan keeps cotton where it does well, as the outer shirts and sweater, and puts wool or fleece where warmth matters.

Warm layer (top)

Best overall: Minus33 Chocorua Midweight Crew (style 705), 100% merino, XL best pick
About $80–90. 235 g/m² merino, which is the weight guides recommend for general cold-weather use. Machine washable, tagless, and the brand says it doesn't itch. Wear it under any of the cotton shirts or the sweater in the cart below: indoors it disappears, outdoors it does the heavy lifting. One shirt can be worn several days in a row without smelling, which also saves carry-on space.

4. Denmark wedding plan

The window: land Frankfurt Dec 25, fly home Jan 12. Danish town halls are mostly closed Christmas to New Year, so the target is about Jan 5 to 8: 2 days in Copenhagen, by plane (~1.5 h) or train.

Why Denmark, not Germany: Germany wants a certificate of no impediment, which Ukraine doesn't issue, so it would need a German high-court exemption (weeks to months, many translations). Denmark needs no residency, accepts English papers, approves in about 5 working days if complete, and costs 2,100 DKK (~280 EUR, Graham pays). A Danish marriage is recognized in the US. Ukraine in person is possible, but Graham is more comfortable with Denmark.

How it works: everything online first, both sign the application; once approved, book a town-hall date and show up. The ceremony takes about 15 minutes. Both must be there in person.

Deadline: send the application by Nov 6 (8+ weeks ahead), because a missing paper can cost weeks and no town hall can be booked before approval.

5. Frankfurt equivalents and what to know

The one that bites: shops are closed on Sundays and holidays. That means Dec 25, Dec 26 and Jan 1 are all fully closed, and Dec 24 and Dec 31 close around 2 pm. You land on Christmas Day, so stock up at the airport, or at the shops in Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof, which are open Sundays and holidays. Jan 6 is a normal day in Hesse.

There is no 24/7 Walmart. German law caps shop hours. Big supermarkets (REWE, Kaufland) are open Mon to Sat until about 10 pm or midnight, and that is the latest. After hours or on a Sunday your options are: the Hauptbahnhof and airport shops (long hours, every day), gas-station shops (Tankstelle, often 24 h, basics at high prices), and Frankfurt's little kiosks (Wasserhäuschen / Trinkhalle) for drinks and snacks. Everything else: plan ahead, or amazon.de.
