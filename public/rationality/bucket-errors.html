Bucket Errors

Untangle what you're really asking — separate the evidence from the conclusion.

What is a Bucket Error?

A bucket error happens when your brain treats two different questions as if they're the same question — dumping evidence for one into the "bucket" of the other.

  • Specific fact: "I made a mistake at work today."
  • Big conclusion: "I'm bad at my job."

These feel like the same thought, but they're answerable by completely different evidence. Tangling them makes you over-update on single events.

Step 1 — Describe the triggering event

What specific thing happened (or might happen) that's bugging you?

Good specifics: "I blanked during the presentation" · "She didn't text back for 6 hours" · "I ate fast food three days in a row"

Step 2 — What big question is your brain jumping to?

When that event happened, what larger conclusion did your mind leap toward? This is usually an identity-level or relationship-level question.

Common big-question patterns:
"Am I [negative identity]?" · "Will [catastrophic outcome] happen?" · "Does [person] actually [feel negative thing] about me?"

Step 3 — Why does your brain connect them?

What's the implicit logic? Why does the specific event feel like evidence for the big conclusion? (It's okay if the logic feels flimsy — write it anyway.)

This step often reveals the hidden assumption that makes the bucket error feel so convincing.

Your Untangled Buckets

Now what?

With the buckets separated, you can:

  • Handle Bucket A on its own terms — learn from the specific event without it meaning anything bigger.
  • Evaluate Bucket B honestly — gather actual evidence across many situations before drawing a conclusion.
  • Notice the pattern — next time your brain tangles them, you'll catch it faster.
