Hinge match quality: Madison vs Austin — final report

Built overnight Oct 6–7, 2026. Names and identifying details of individual matches are omitted.

Verdict (3 sentences)

On blind photo attractiveness, Austin matches scored higher than Madison matches: 5.7 vs 5.1 on a 10-point scale (difference 0.6 points, 95% CI 0.1 to 1.1, p = 0.015), and the gap held for both raters, for face and body separately, and after dropping low-confidence profiles. Part of that gap is probably selection rather than city, because the Austin list had already been pruned by Graham's own looks-based unmatching for months, while the Madison list was fresh and unpruned, so this is the upper bound of the real city difference. On everything else that gave both cities an equal shot (age, kids and family plans, intentions, substance use, profile completeness, how much she wrote back), the two cities are statistically indistinguishable.

3. Excluded metrics, and why

  • Dates or meetups: the Madison stay was two weeks and busy with family, so Madison had almost no chance to convert.
  • Count of his 9s and 10s, or any of his ratings: Madison was never systematically rated. His ratings appear only as the calibration check above.
  • Raw match counts: depend on days active, filters and hidden chats.
  • "Replied at all": biased by Hinge hiding chats after 14 days (Austin non-responders were hidden). Reported in the table but not used.
  • Who initiated and response latency: not recoverable, because message order was scrambled during capture.
  • "Asked where he lives" or "bailed over Austin": only possible in Madison.
  • Photo count: Hinge requires 6 photos, so it carries no signal.

4. Method

  • Population: all 54 matches in Graham's list as of Oct 1 (38 Madison, 16 Austin). City is Graham's Hinge location when the match happened (Madison Sep 17 – Oct 1, Austin otherwise).
  • Photo capture (Oct 6): read-only over USB adb. For each match, opened the chat (text saved) and the Profile tab, then screenshotted while scrolling. Identity was confirmed against age and job from the Oct 1 capture, because several first names are duplicated. Final coverage is 47 of 54 originals with usable photos:
  • Madison 31 of 38. Six were no longer in the list on Oct 6, and one has only a group photo.
  • Austin 16 of 16.
  • Blinding:
  • Every photo was auto-cropped out of the screenshot, which removes name, age, location and all text.
  • Near-duplicate crops were removed.
  • Each person's crops were shuffled onto one sheet named with a random ID; the mapping was sealed in a separate file.
  • Raters saw only the sheets and the rubric, never names, city or each other's scores.
  • Scores were joined back only after both raters finished, and no score was changed after unblinding.
  • Rubric: blind2/rubric.md, fixed before any scoring. Anchors: 5 = average for women 30-45 on a dating app, 7 = top 25%, 8 = top 10%, 9 = top 3%.
  • Statistics: bootstrap CIs, permutation and Mann-Whitney tests, Cliff's delta for effect size, and Holm correction across the 24 profile-field tests.

5. Assumptions and checks

  1. Survivorship by pruning (the main caveat). Graham routinely unmatches Austin matches he'd rate 6 or below, and inactive Austin chats were hidden by Hinge. The 16 Austin matches are survivors of months of looks-based filtering; the Madison 38 are a fresh, unfiltered batch. That alone could produce a gap like 0.6 points, so the looks result is an upper bound on the true city effect. It can't be fully separated with this data.
  2. Both lists were also screened at like-time. In Madison, part of the matching was the AI assistant's autonomous Discover run (like 7 or higher on appearance, Sep 23-24); in Austin, Graham chose. Different screeners add noise but no obvious direction.
  3. Madison losses: 6 of 38 were gone by Oct 6, after Graham's back-in-Austin note. If those were systematically more or less attractive the Madison mean could shift, but 6 people can't move a 31-person mean by 0.6 points unless they averaged about 9, and the highest Madison score was 7.
  4. Pool composition: Madison's 40-mile radius reaches small towns, but 75% vs 80% live within 15 miles. The Sep 24 age-filter fix means early Madison matches include women in their 40s. The blind rubric doesn't adjust for age, and mean ages were similar (37.3 vs 38.0).
  5. Model raters compress the scale (4-7 only) and match Graham's taste only modestly (rho 0.55). Treat the looks numbers as consensus attractiveness, not his preference.
  6. Small n: 31 vs 16. The looks result clears p < 0.05 and holds under every robustness cut, but the CI is wide (0.1 to 1.1 points).
  7. Profile-field tests: 24 tests, none survive Holm correction (see section 2).
  8. Lower bar in Madison (Graham, Oct 7). Knowing the Madison window was only two weeks, Graham says he was much less selective about who he liked or accepted there. Together with item 1 (Austin pruned) and item 2 (some Madison likes came from the AI assistant's autonomous run), all three selection effects push the same way: lower looks in Madison regardless of the city. The 0.6-point gap may be mostly or entirely selection.
  9. Matches measure the filter, not the city. Every match passed the same Hinge filters (age, distance, intentions) and Graham's consistent taste, so the profile-field "tie" is expected by construction and says little about the underlying pools. A real city comparison needs fresh, unfiltered Discover batches from each city, scored blind before anyone likes or prunes.

6. Bottom line for decisions

If the question is "is the Austin dating pool better than Madison's," the honest answer is: about the same on values, goals and engagement, and somewhat better-looking in Austin, but by less than this data shows, because Graham's Austin list was pre-filtered for looks. A clean test would compare fresh Discover batches in each city, scored blind before anyone likes or prunes.
