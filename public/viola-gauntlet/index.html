Viola Gauntlet

Seven daily practice routines for viola, one for each day of the week, rotating. Each one is a scrolling-sheet-music video with a backing track that plays along: drones to tune against, piano and strings under the scales and melodies, a light groove for the bowing work. Play along with the video; the score scrolls with a playhead and every exercise starts with two bars of count-in clicks.

Every day opens with the same core (about 7 minutes, in that day's key): open-string long tones over a drone for bow control, drone intonation on the day's scale, and perfect fifths across strings as a hand-frame check. Then comes the day's focus. Items marked stretch are deliberately past today's comfort zone: aim at them, don't force them.

Day 1 · Monday: Scales & arpeggios

Key of the day: C major / C melodic minor · 22 min · 11 exercises

Three-octave scales and the Flesch arpeggio cycle in C, slow bows first, then faster slurs. The drone and a soft string chord sit under every scale so you can hear intonation, not just feel it.

Watch on the video site Printable part (PDF) Backing track only (mp3)

  1. Open-string long tones (3 min)
    One unbroken tone per bow, in tune with the drone; middle sounding point, full even tone

    Bow control and tone on the four open strings, each tuned against a low drone an octave below. First an 8-beat bow each way swelling p to mf and back, then 12-beat bows as a messa di voce (pp, swell to f, back to pp). Today's sounding point: middle sounding point, full even tone. Soft clicks keep the beat so you can divide the bow into equal parts (a quarter of the bow per beat on the 8-beat bows).

    Stretch: 12-beat bows with no audible bow change

  2. Drone intonation (2 min)
    Each note rings with the drone - no beats; thirds low, leading note high

    A two-octave scale in half notes over a drone of the tonic and fifth, so every note can be heard as an interval against the drone. Then an interval ladder: tonic, then each scale degree in turn, then the notes borrowed from the parallel minor. Tune until the sound goes still (no beating): major thirds and sixths sit a little lower than the piano, the leading note leans up.

  3. Fifths across strings (2 min)
    One finger flat across two strings: a pure fifth means the hand frame is right

    Perfect fifths stopped by a single finger lying across two strings, every finger, every string pair, in the notes of today's key. Sustain the double stop until it is pure (the drone gives the lower note), then play it broken: lower, upper, both. A fifth that won't come pure means the finger is not square across both strings, so it diagnoses the hand frame: fix the finger's angle rather than squeezing.

  4. C major scale, 3 octaves (3 min)
    Even tone and exact shifts from C3 to C6 and back; bow speed matched to slurs

    The C major scale over three octaves (C3 to C6), five passes: whole bows one note per bow (half notes), then 2, 4 and 8 notes per bow as the rhythm doubles. Standard Flesch fingering: shift on the same finger pattern every octave (1-2-3 / 1-2-3-4, shifts after the 3rd finger going up). A C drone and a soft C major string chord sit underneath.

    Stretch: 16ths at q=80, 8 per bow, without rushing the top octave

  5. C melodic minor, 3 octaves (3 min)
    Raised 6th and 7th going up, natural going down; keep the minor third low

    C melodic minor over three octaves, same five passes as the major scale. Up: A natural and B natural; down: B flat and A flat. The minor third (E flat) and the minor sixth coming down sit low against the drone.

    Stretch: 16ths at q=80 with the top octave as clean as the bottom

  6. Arpeggio cycle (Flesch), 3 octaves (3 min)
    Six arpeggios that all start on C: frame the hand, shift on the open finger

    Carl Flesch's arpeggio cycle: six arpeggios that all begin on low C - C major, C minor, A flat major (first inversion), A minor (first inversion), F minor and F major (second inversion) - each three octaves up and back. First slurred 3 per bow, then 6 per bow. A soft string chord and low cello note give the harmony.

    Stretch: 6 per bow at this tempo; land C6 in tune every time

  7. Seventh arpeggios, 3 octaves (2 min)
    Four notes per octave: the hand frame changes shape, the shifts stay quiet

    The dominant seventh (C E G B flat) and diminished seventh (C E flat G flat A) arpeggios on C, three octaves: separate bows, 4 per bow, then 8 per bow in sixteenths.

    Stretch: 16th-note pass, 8 per bow, through C6

  8. Broken thirds, 2 octaves (1.0 min)
    Finger pairs land together; hear each third against the drone

    The C major scale in broken thirds (C-E, D-F, E-G...) up two octaves and back, slurred 2 then 4 per bow. Preparation for Wednesday's double-stop thirds.

  9. Scales on one string (1.4 min)
    A whole octave on ONE string: shifts by 1-2-3, 1-2-3-4, slow and slide-free

    One octave of C major notes on each string alone (C string from C, G string from G, D string from D, A string from A), so every note change is a shift. Use 1-2-3-1-2-3-4 style fingerings and keep the shifts silent - release finger pressure, move, then put weight back.

    Stretch: the A string octave (A4 to A5) clean, no audible slides

  10. Chromatic scale, 2 octaves (1.1 min)
    Tiny, even half steps; the fingers move like a zipper, not a slide

    Two octaves of chromatic scale from C3 to C5 and back (sharps up, flats down), 4 then 8 per bow. Half steps on the viola are wide - keep them even, and keep the hand frame still while the fingers do the work.

  11. Performance pass, with a groove (1.2 min)
    Play the scales like music: steady 16ths, big tone, locked to the bass

    The two three-octave scales once more at a brisk tempo in sixteenths, now over a light groove (pizzicato bass and off-beat piano chords): detache, then 4 and 8 per bow. This is the 'play it like it matters' pass.

    Stretch: all six passes at q=84 without a stumble

Day 4 · Thursday: Bowing

Key of the day: F major · 23 min · 12 exercises

One short F major figure played with a different bow stroke each time: detache, legato, martele, hooked rhythms, colle, string crossings, spiccato and sautille. The left hand stays easy so the whole day is about the right arm, over a light groove.

Watch on the video site Printable part (PDF) Backing track only (mp3)

  1. Open-string long tones (3 min)
    One unbroken tone per bow, in tune with the drone; near the fingerboard: light and fast, then back to the middle

    Bow control and tone on the four open strings, each tuned against a low drone an octave below. First an 8-beat bow each way swelling p to mf and back, then 12-beat bows held at an even pp (son file). Today's sounding point: near the fingerboard: light and fast, then back to the middle. Soft clicks keep the beat so you can divide the bow into equal parts (a quarter of the bow per beat on the 8-beat bows).

    Stretch: 12-beat bows with no audible bow change

  2. Drone intonation (2 min)
    Each note rings with the drone - no beats; thirds low, leading note high

    A two-octave scale in half notes over a drone of the tonic and fifth, so every note can be heard as an interval against the drone. Then an interval ladder: tonic, then each scale degree in turn, then the notes borrowed from the parallel minor. Tune until the sound goes still (no beating): major thirds and sixths sit a little lower than the piano, the leading note leans up.

  3. Fifths across strings (2 min)
    One finger flat across two strings: a pure fifth means the hand frame is right

    Perfect fifths stopped by a single finger lying across two strings, every finger, every string pair, in the notes of today's key. Sustain the double stop until it is pure (the drone gives the lower note), then play it broken: lower, upper, both. A fifth that won't come pure means the finger is not square across both strings, so it diagnoses the hand frame: fix the finger's angle rather than squeezing.

  4. Detache: middle and whole bow (3 min)
    One note per bow, no gaps, no accents: the bow changes are invisible

    Cues: M = middle of the bow, WB = whole bow, UH = upper half, LH = lower half, Fr = at the frog. The day's figure: eight bars of F major sixteenths in first position (F, B flat, C7, F, then D minor, G minor, C7, F), played three ways. First detache in the middle of the bow, a few inches of bow per note, smooth and connected. Then grand detache in eighths using the whole bow for every note - full speed, even sound from frog to tip, the arm opening and closing from the elbow. Finally detache again in the upper half, lighter. Keep the sounding point steady and make every bow change sound the same in both directions. A light groove (pizzicato bass and off-beat piano) keeps the pulse.

  5. Legato slurs: 4, 8 and 16 (2 min)
    Long slurs with an even bow speed; string crossings rolled, not bumped

    The same figure slurred 4, then 8, then 16 notes per bow (a whole bar in one bow). Divide the bow evenly over the slur so the last notes are not squeezed, and roll the arm level smoothly between strings so the crossings sound like part of the line. Only the left hand articulates the notes - the fingers drop firmly so each note speaks under the slur. A soft string chord sits under it.

    Stretch: 16 notes per bow at q=60 with full tone to the end of each bow

  6. Martele: bite and stop (2 min)
    Pinch the string, release with speed, stop: clean silence between notes

    Cues: M = middle of the bow, WB = whole bow, UH = upper half, LH = lower half, Fr = at the frog. Martele (marked, hammered stroke). In the upper half: set the bow on the string with weight (index finger), release it with a fast stroke that starts with a clean consonant, then stop the bow on the string - the silence between notes is part of the stroke. First in eighths through the whole figure, then a smaller martele in sixteenths in the middle of the bow on the first phrase. No crunch at the start: the bite comes from pressure released, not pressed harder.

    Stretch: the 16th-note martele at q=60, each stroke still stopped

  7. Hooked bowing, dotted rhythms (2 min)
    Dotted 8th and 16th in the same bow; the short note stays short and clear

    The figure in dotted rhythms (dotted eighth + sixteenth). First with separate bows, which teaches bow distribution: slow bow on the long note, fast and light on the short one so you don't drift to the tip. Then hooked: the dotted eighth and the sixteenth in the SAME bow direction with a small stop between them (down-down, up-up). Keep the rhythm crisp - the sixteenth is late and short, never a lazy triplet.

  8. Colle (optional quick drill) (0.9 min)
    At the frog: a tiny pinched click, then lift the bow off the string

    Cues: M = middle of the bow, WB = whole bow, UH = upper half, LH = lower half, Fr = at the frog. Colle (glued): optional, about a minute. At the frog, place the bow on the string, pinch it with the fingers so the string 'catches', release with a tiny fast finger-and-wrist stroke, and lift off in the rest. Alternating bows first, then all down-bows with a lift and reset between notes. This is the finger action behind every clean bow start and behind spiccato later today.

  9. String crossings: bariolage (2 min)
    Fingered note against the open string: tiny level changes, from wrist and fingers

    Cues: M = middle of the bow, WB = whole bow, UH = upper half, LH = lower half, Fr = at the frog. Bariolage: a fingered note on the lower string alternating with the open string above it, first D against open A, then G-string notes against open D. Separate bows first (small strokes in the middle, the forearm and wrist making the crossing), then slurred, where only the wrist and fingers rock the bow - the arm stays at a level between the two strings. Open strings ring; keep them as loud as the fingered notes, not louder. The harmony is chosen so the open string always belongs to the chord.

    Stretch: the slurred 8s across G-D with no bumps on the open D

  10. Spiccato, lower half (2 min)
    Off the string: each note is a small dropped arc, clear and even

    Cues: M = middle of the bow, WB = whole bow, UH = upper half, LH = lower half, Fr = at the frog. Spiccato in the lower half of the bow (find the spot a little below the balance point where the bow bounces most easily). The arm makes small U-shaped arcs: the bow drops into the string and leaves it again on every note. First in eighths, then in sixteenths with smaller, lower arcs - the faster it goes, the closer the bow stays to the string. Listen for a clear 'tock' with pitch, not a scratch or a splash.

    Stretch: spiccato 16ths at q=72 through the whole figure

  11. Sautille, brisk (0.9 min)
    Fast short detache at the balance point: the stick bounces, you just steer

    Cues: M = middle of the bow, WB = whole bow, UH = upper half, LH = lower half, Fr = at the frog. Sautille: a fast detache in the middle of the bow (at or just above the balance point) where the hair stays close to the string and the stick's own spring makes it bounce. Don't throw the bow - play a small fast detache with a loose hand, tilt the stick slightly and let the bounce appear. Start each pass on the string if it doesn't bounce yet. This is a stretch tempo today: if it falls apart, play the passes on the string at q=100 and keep the hand loose.

    Stretch: the whole exercise: sautille 16ths at q=100

  12. Mixed bowings (Kreutzer 2 style) (2 min)
    Uneven groupings with even sound: plan where the bow is before each group

    The 'performance pass': the figure with the classic Kreutzer No. 2 bowing variants. 2 slurred + 2 separate, 1 separate + 3 slurred, 3 slurred + 1 separate. These groupings pull the bow toward one end: use more bow (faster) for the slurs and less for the separate notes so you stay in the middle. Play it like music, with a big tone, over the groove.

    Stretch: all three patterns at q=66 without drifting to the frog or tip

    The whole etude at half speed: every note in tune, every crossing clean

The short note forces the next finger (and every shift) to be ready early, so when you go back to even sixteenths they feel easy. Keep the dots sharp. Soft piano chords follow the harmony.

Day 7 · Sunday: Lyrical playing & repertoire

Key of the day: B flat major / G minor · 19 min · 7 exercises

A lighter, musical day: an aria, a 5/4 waltz and a sarabande, all original and all with a piano and string accompanist under you. Phrasing is the point today - shape every phrase like a breath, then close with quiet long tones on the C string.

  4. Aria in B flat (cantabile) (5 min)
    Sing through every phrase, breathe at the rests; the bow is your breath

    An original aria in B flat, 32 bars in four 8-bar breaths (A, A again, a climbing middle section, A quietly), played twice. Mostly first position on the D and A strings; bars 17-22 climb into 3rd position to a G5 at forte (written in treble clef). Each phrase ends with a rest marked 'breathe': let the bow lift, breathe in, and start the next phrase as if singing it. Slurs are bow strokes - use the whole bow on the half notes, keep the sound alive to the end of each note, and follow the hairpins. A piano plays broken chords with a soft string pad under you; on the first pass a quiet solo viola touches the top G (bar 20) as a target to land on. Second time through, take more freedom.

    Stretch: the climb to G5 in 3rd position at forte, in tune and still singing

  5. Waltz in five (G minor, 3 + 2) (4 min)
    Feel 5/4 as 3 + 2: a long swing, then a short one - never count to five

    An original waltz in G minor in 5/4, played twice: each bar is a waltz bar of three beats plus a short bar of two, so it limps gracefully, like a waltz with a missing beat. Most bars are a long note or three quarters (the 3) and then two quarters or a half note (the 2). The piano plays it that way too: bass on beats 1 and 4, chords on 2, 3 and 5, with a soft string pad. Stay in first position (top note E flat 5); breathe on the half-bar rests, lean a little into beats 1 and 4, and let the last note fade to nothing.

    Stretch: feel the 3 + 2 in the body, not by counting, both times through

  6. Sarabande with double stops (2 min)
    Slow 3/4 that leans on beat 2; double stops ring with an open string

    An original sarabande in B flat, 16 bars played twice (second time softer). A sarabande is a slow dance whose weight falls on beat 2, so most bars put a dotted-quarter double stop there: let it bloom, then release. The double stops are easy ones in first position, most with an open string (D with B flat, open D with open A, C with open A), plus a few sixths and one fifth under one finger (E flat with B flat, bar 14). Tune the stopped note to the open string until both ring. A piano holds each chord with a soft string pad.

  7. Cool-down: C string and a last scale (2 min)
    Long, quiet bows on the C string, then let the scale come home to B flat

    A quiet close. Over a low B flat and F drone, long whole-bar bows on the C string: D (a sweet major third over the drone - keep it low), F (a pure fifth), E flat and D again, each swelling a little and fading. Then one slow octave of B flat major down from the A string, two notes per bow, ending on a soft sixth (D on the C string with B flat on the G string) held to nothing. Let the shoulders drop; this is the exhale of the week.

How to use it

  • Do the day that matches the weekday, or just the next one in the rotation. Missing a day is fine; keep the order.
  • Tune to the drone, not to the piano. Long tones and the fifths are the warm-up; don't skip them.
  • If an exercise is too fast, slow the video down in your player (most players do 0.75x) and come back to full speed later.
  • The PDF has every exercise without the count-ins, for practising a spot on its own. The mp3 is the backing alone, same timeline as the video.
