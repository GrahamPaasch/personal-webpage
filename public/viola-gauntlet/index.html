Viola Gauntlet

Seven daily practice routines for viola, one for each day of the week, rotating. Each one is a scrolling-sheet-music video with a backing track that plays along: drones to tune against, piano and strings under the scales and melodies, a light groove for the bowing work. Play along with the video; the score scrolls with a playhead and every exercise starts with two bars of count-in clicks.

Every day opens with the same core (about 7 minutes, in that day's key): open-string long tones over a drone for bow control, drone intonation on the day's scale, and perfect fifths across strings as a hand-frame check. Then comes the day's focus. Items marked stretch are deliberately past today's comfort zone: aim at them, don't force them.

Day 1 · Monday: Scales & arpeggios

Key of the day: C major / C melodic minor · 22 min · 11 exercises

Three-octave scales and the Flesch arpeggio cycle in C, slow bows first, then faster slurs. The drone and a soft string chord sit under every scale so you can hear intonation, not just feel it.

Watch on the video site Printable part (PDF) Backing track only (mp3)

  1. Open-string long tones (3 min)
    One unbroken tone per bow, in tune with the drone; middle sounding point, full even tone

    Bow control and tone on the four open strings, each tuned against a low drone an octave below. First an 8-beat bow each way swelling p to mf and back, then 12-beat bows as a messa di voce (pp, swell to f, back to pp). Today's sounding point: middle sounding point, full even tone. Soft clicks keep the beat so you can divide the bow into equal parts (a quarter of the bow per beat on the 8-beat bows).

    Stretch: 12-beat bows with no audible bow change

  2. Drone intonation (2 min)
    Each note rings with the drone - no beats; thirds low, leading note high

    A two-octave scale in half notes over a drone of the tonic and fifth, so every note can be heard as an interval against the drone. Then an interval ladder: tonic, then each scale degree in turn, then the notes borrowed from the parallel minor. Tune until the sound goes still (no beating): major thirds and sixths sit a little lower than the piano, the leading note leans up.

  3. Fifths across strings (2 min)
    One finger flat across two strings: a pure fifth means the hand frame is right

    Perfect fifths stopped by a single finger lying across two strings, every finger, every string pair, in the notes of today's key. Sustain the double stop until it is pure (the drone gives the lower note), then play it broken: lower, upper, both. A fifth that won't come pure means the finger is not square across both strings, so it diagnoses the hand frame: fix the finger's angle rather than squeezing.

  4. C major scale, 3 octaves (3 min)
    Even tone and exact shifts from C3 to C6 and back; bow speed matched to slurs

    The C major scale over three octaves (C3 to C6), five passes: whole bows one note per bow (half notes), then 2, 4 and 8 notes per bow as the rhythm doubles. Standard Flesch fingering: shift on the same finger pattern every octave (1-2-3 / 1-2-3-4, shifts after the 3rd finger going up). A C drone and a soft C major string chord sit underneath.

    Stretch: 16ths at q=80, 8 per bow, without rushing the top octave

  5. C melodic minor, 3 octaves (3 min)
    Raised 6th and 7th going up, natural going down; keep the minor third low

    C melodic minor over three octaves, same five passes as the major scale. Up: A natural and B natural; down: B flat and A flat. The minor third (E flat) and the minor sixth coming down sit low against the drone.

    Stretch: 16ths at q=80 with the top octave as clean as the bottom

  6. Arpeggio cycle (Flesch), 3 octaves (3 min)
    Six arpeggios that all start on C: frame the hand, shift on the open finger

    Carl Flesch's arpeggio cycle: six arpeggios that all begin on low C - C major, C minor, A flat major (first inversion), A minor (first inversion), F minor and F major (second inversion) - each three octaves up and back. First slurred 3 per bow, then 6 per bow. A soft string chord and low cello note give the harmony.

    Stretch: 6 per bow at this tempo; land C6 in tune every time

  7. Seventh arpeggios, 3 octaves (2 min)
    Four notes per octave: the hand frame changes shape, the shifts stay quiet

    The dominant seventh (C E G B flat) and diminished seventh (C E flat G flat A) arpeggios on C, three octaves: separate bows, 4 per bow, then 8 per bow in sixteenths.

    Stretch: 16th-note pass, 8 per bow, through C6

  8. Broken thirds, 2 octaves (1.0 min)
    Finger pairs land together; hear each third against the drone

    The C major scale in broken thirds (C-E, D-F, E-G...) up two octaves and back, slurred 2 then 4 per bow. Preparation for Wednesday's double-stop thirds.

  9. Scales on one string (1.4 min)
    A whole octave on ONE string: shifts by 1-2-3, 1-2-3-4, slow and slide-free

    One octave of C major notes on each string alone (C string from C, G string from G, D string from D, A string from A), so every note change is a shift. Use 1-2-3-1-2-3-4 style fingerings and keep the shifts silent - release finger pressure, move, then put weight back.

    Stretch: the A string octave (A4 to A5) clean, no audible slides

  10. Chromatic scale, 2 octaves (1.1 min)
    Tiny, even half steps; the fingers move like a zipper, not a slide

    Two octaves of chromatic scale from C3 to C5 and back (sharps up, flats down), 4 then 8 per bow. Half steps on the viola are wide - keep them even, and keep the hand frame still while the fingers do the work.

  11. Performance pass, with a groove (1.2 min)
    Play the scales like music: steady 16ths, big tone, locked to the bass

    The two three-octave scales once more at a brisk tempo in sixteenths, now over a light groove (pizzicato bass and off-beat piano chords): detache, then 4 and 8 per bow. This is the 'play it like it matters' pass.

    Stretch: all six passes at q=84 without a stumble

How to use it

  • Do the day that matches the weekday, or just the next one in the rotation. Missing a day is fine; keep the order.
  • Tune to the drone, not to the piano. Long tones and the fifths are the warm-up; don't skip them.
  • If an exercise is too fast, slow the video down in your player (most players do 0.75x) and come back to full speed later.
  • The PDF has every exercise without the count-ins, for practising a spot on its own. The mp3 is the backing alone, same timeline as the video.
